# Wave 3 — Two New Loops: Division & the Live Stream

Waves 1–2 built the Tetris pillars with six verbs — **enclose, shift, rotate, allocate** (static
fields), then **fuse** and **trigger** (a dynamic physical basin, a loaded spring). Read against the
matrix in `03-prototype-rationale.md` and `04-prototype-wave-2.md`, two cells are still empty:

1. **Every "impose order" so far is either elimination or synthesis.** You *remove* mess (clear the
   fire, the run, the queue) or you *combine* it upward (NOVA's fusion ladder). Nobody **divides** —
   takes one tangled mass and *partitions* it into pure pieces. Cutting is the oldest ordering verb
   there is (sorting, classification, "separate the wheat from the chaff") and we have never tested it.
2. **Every field is discrete and edited in place.** Even NOVA's basin is a pile of settled bodies you
   add to. Nobody routes a **continuous live stream** — a flow that is *already moving* and must be
   *steered*, in analog, before it lands. CONDUIT routes a pulse, but on a grid, on a beat, by snapping
   nodes to 90°. Nobody bends an analog trajectory with a force field.

Wave 3 attacks both. It also deliberately diversifies the **release event** — the dossier's §3.6
prediction-error reward is strongest when the *kind* of catharsis is novel — adding the **sever-shatter**
and the **curved-collect streak** to the arcade's existing pops, cascades, pulses, saves, climbs and
eruptions.

| Prototype | Core verb | Fresh metaphor | New axis it opens | Leans hardest on |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **CLEAVE** | **cut** (slice to partition) | A crystal grows from a colored stream; swipe to cleave pure regions off it | **Order by division** — partition, not eliminate or combine | #2 order-from-chaos (as *separation*), #4 risk/reward release |
| **FLUX** | **bend** (steer a live stream) | Charged particles spray across a field; drop magnetic poles to curve them home | **The live analog stream** — route a flow already in motion, in continuous physics | #3 tractable-under-uncertainty, #1 perpetual pressure |

Both are **one-finger mobile-native** (CLEAVE = swipe a line; FLUX = tap a point) and both are built
as **maximum-juice showcases** — the standing mandate since Wave 2: every input over-delivers a
physical reaction (squash, bloom, shake, chromatic split, pitch-laddered audio, haptics,
time-dilation on the big beats), because reactivity itself is treated as a primary dopamine channel,
not paint.

## CLEAVE — *the division loop*

**Verb:** **swipe** a straight line across the chamber to cut. **Generator:** a crystal grows up from
the floor — new cells crystallise onto its surface every tick, each a random colour (the disordered
input stream). **Completion:** a cut severs every bond its line crosses, splitting the crystal into
connected components; any component that is **monochrome and ≥ K cells** *shatters* — scores, clears,
relieves height — while mixed remainders fall and re-settle. A single well-aimed diagonal can shatter
several pure regions at once (a **combo**). **Loss:** the crystal grows past the ceiling line.

- **Order from chaos (2), as *division*:** the chaos is a mottled, multi-colour mass; your verb
  imposes order by *separating like from unlike*. This is the one ordering fantasy the arcade has
  never rendered — not removing the mess, not fusing it, but **partitioning** it. The satisfaction is
  the clean break: a tangle resolves into pure shards.
- **Risk/reward = the Tetris gamble (4, 6):** cut early for guaranteed small monochrome harvests, or
  let a single colour spread into a huge region (while the crystal climbs toward the ceiling) for one
  massive shatter. The exact "safe single vs. greedy Tetris" decision shape — now expressed as *when
  do I cut*, governed by growth.
- **Transparent + fair (5, 7):** the whole crystal and every colour boundary are always visible; the
  connected-component rule is deterministic and legible — you can *see* the cut you need. Uncertainty
  lives only in the next colour the stream crystallises.
- **Tractable under uncertainty (3):** each cut is one obvious "isolate that blue lobe"; planning a
  diagonal that purifies *both* sides at once, or sculpting a region to grow huge before cleaving it,
  is deep and high-skilled.
- **Self-scaling (8):** the single knob is **growth rate**, which ramps with score — the better you
  do, the faster it climbs, the less time you have to set up the big cut.
- **The new release — the *sever-shatter*:** a swipe should feel like cutting taut silk. The blade
  flashes along the full line, the two halves recoil apart along the cut normal, the seam sprays
  sparks, and each purified region detonates in its own colour with an ascending chime — a bisection
  followed by a bloom. A distinct catharsis from any pop, cascade or eruption already in the arcade.

## FLUX — *the live-stream loop*

**Verb:** **tap** an empty point to drop a **magnetic pole** that curves nearby particles; poles fade
after a few seconds (and only a few live at once). **Generator:** emitters on the rim spray charged,
colour-coded particles across the field in continuous motion (the disordered stream — *already
moving*, unlike every prior field). **Completion:** a particle that enters its **matching-colour
collector** banks; rapid banks build a **streak multiplier** and the collector erupts when a streak
caps. **Loss:** particles that miss, or hit the *wrong* collector, raise a **spill meter** to
overflow.

- **The live analog stream (new):** every other prototype edits a *still* field (even NOVA adds to a
  settling pile). FLUX hands you a flow that is **already in motion** and asks you to *bend its
  future*, in continuous physics, before it lands. This is the most "real-time analog" relationship to
  order the arcade has — closer to juggling water than to placing a brick.
- **Tractable under uncertainty (3, 6):** each tap is one obvious "curve that clump toward its bin";
  planning a single pole that slings *three* colours past *three* collectors in one arc, or chaining
  poles into a steering corridor, is deep. The macro-problem (a continuous routing optimisation under a
  moving load) is genuinely hard while every atomic tap is legible.
- **Perpetual pressure (1) + loss-aversion (§4):** the spray never stops; an idle player's stream runs
  straight off the field and the spill meter climbs. The felt motive is *don't let it spill* — the same
  top-out aversion that drives Tetris, rendered as a leaking flow.
- **Transparent + fair (5, 7):** every particle's velocity and every collector's colour are visible;
  pole influence is shown as a field ring. Trajectories are deterministic — a spill is always a pole
  you placed late or wrong, never the system cheating. Uncertainty is only in the next emission.
- **Self-scaling (8):** emit rate (and particle speed) ramp with score — one knob, more flow.
- **The new release — the *curved-collect streak*:** the catharsis is a **sustained** one — a long,
  glowing, curved trail of light whipping around a pole and snapping into its bin with a colour flash
  and a rising pitch, again and again, until a collector overflows in a banked eruption. Where SURGE
  withholds and releases once, FLUX pays a *flowing* stream of small, escalating releases — a different
  shape of dopamine to test against the held breath.

## What Wave 3 adds to the testing matrix

- **Elimination vs. synthesis vs. division:** the originals *remove* mess, NOVA *combines* it, **CLEAVE**
  *partitions* it. Three complete, distinct fantasies of "imposing order" — the cleanest three-way
  contrast in the arcade.
- **Edited field vs. settling pile vs. live stream:** static (waves 1) → dynamic-but-settling (NOVA) →
  **continuously-flowing** (FLUX). FLUX is the first field that *moves on its own with direction*, not
  just gravity.
- **One verb that is a *gesture*, not a tap or hold:** CLEAVE's swipe-line is the arcade's first true
  *stroke* input — a continuous gesture whose *angle and position* both carry meaning. A new input
  grammar to test for mobile satisfaction.
- **Withheld single release (SURGE) vs. flowing escalating release (FLUX):** two opposite answers to
  "what shape of catharsis craves hardest" — the loaded spring vs. the singing stream.
- **Juice as a primary channel (continued):** both over-deliver on every input. CLEAVE's whole appeal
  is the *sever* — a gesture-locked, gravity-recoiled, spark-seamed bisection — and FLUX's is the
  *trail* — analog light bending through a force field. Each is engineered to be the most satisfying
  instance of its verb we can render in 2D.

Each ships as a standalone zero-dependency HTML file (`public/cleave.html`, `public/flux.html`) with a
live score/pressure readout, WebAudio tension–release cues, haptics where supported, and a
fail→instant-restart loop. Both pass the "is it Tetris-shaped?" acceptance test
(`02-design-pillars.md`) while testing a relationship to *order* — division, and the steering of a live
flow — that the first six never did.

## The juice log — six cycles per game

The standing mandate (Wave 2) is that **reactivity is a primary dopamine channel**. After each game's
loop was built and its core algorithm verified deterministically (in-browser, via a `window.__cleave`
/ `window.__flux` debug hook and a headless autoplayer used to *balance* difficulty before any polish),
six dedicated game-feel cycles were run — each a focused pass that goes beyond the delivery of the
earlier arcade.

### CLEAVE — the sever-shatter
1. **The blade.** A bright glint sweeps the full chamber span of every cut; the crystal recoils apart
   along the cut normal (falloff by distance from the line); the seam sprays sparks. The gesture *reads*
   as cutting taut silk, hit or miss.
2. **The shatter.** Each harvested cell bursts into spinning gem-shards (color + highlight facet) under
   gravity, over twin rings (color + white). Big shatters (≥6) trigger a **hit-stop freeze** then
   ease-out **slow-motion** and a screen flash scaled to the payoff — the catharsis lands physically.
3. **Audio identity.** A glassy descending slice "shhk"; crystalline shatter arpeggios that ladder up
   in pitch with vein size and combo order; a rising **combo sweep**; a low *settle thud* timed to the
   remainder falling; a danger hum that pulses as the chamber fills; sub-audible growth ticks.
4. **Anticipation.** An **incoming-shard ghost** (diamond + beam) telegraphs which column the next cell
   lands in; columns flash on receive; the cut **preview** highlights exactly which veins will shatter
   and floats the **predicted score** (`+1672 ×3`) at the cursor; the ceiling pulses faster as it fills.
5. **Readability.** Per-cell **vein boundary rims** make colour regions pop; an idle shimmer sweeps the
   field; a centred `N× COMBO` banner on multi-cleaves; HUD value bumps; gem facet shading.
6. **Mobile & feel.** Size/combo-scaled **haptic** patterns; a finger trail along the swipe; a
   first-time "swipe to cut" demo stroke (shown once, cleared on first cut); `touch-action:none` so the
   gesture never scrolls the page; a `prefers-reduced-motion` guard that drops shake and slow-mo.

### FLUX — the curved-collect streak
1. **Magnetism, visible.** Each pole shows a soft field gradient *and* an animated dashed **reach ring**
   so its influence radius is legible, plus **pull-lines** curving from every steered particle to its
   pole — the *bend* is drawn, not implied.
2. **Light ribbons.** Particles trail graded additive ribbons that thicken and brighten toward the head;
   a fresh particle draws an entry streak from the emitter line. The field reads as flowing light.
3. **Bank & streak escalation.** Banks pop a colour burst + ring + float; the collector lip brightens; a
   pitch-laddered bank chime rises with the streak; a **multiplier-up banner**; and every tenth bank a
   screen-wide **SURGE** eruption with a bonus.
4. **Anticipation / readability.** The collector under a held magnet is **highlighted** (you see where
   particles will land); each bin glows as a matching particle descends toward it; bin labels light on
   approach — so intent is always legible (Pillar 5).
5. **Spill pressure.** A red vignette and low hum rise with the spill; the collectors **rattle** past
   70%; banking visibly relieves it — pressure you can feel and recover from.
6. **Mobile & feel.** The core verb is **hold-and-drag** (one finger): touch drops a weighted magnet you
   slide to aim and lift to leave fading behind; grab gives a ripple + haptic; bank/miss/surge each have
   distinct vibration; first-time hint; `prefers-reduced-motion` guard.

Difficulty for both was tuned against headless autoplayers, not guesswork: CLEAVE settles a *competent*
player around 50 % chamber height with recoverable spikes (a clean board is the skill reward); FLUX's
single-finger pole keeps the spill in a tense band that a careless player overflows. Both are designed
so an idle player's default trajectory is **loss** (Pillar 1).

> **Update — the petrification loss loop.** A first pass made CLEAVE (like NOVA/SURGE) too hard to
> *lose*: a fully-clearable field plus action-efficient cuts let a skilled player hold the board near
> empty forever. CLEAVE now has a **calcification ratchet** — shards left too long turn to uncuttable
> grey **stone** (only knocked loose by shattering an adjacent vein), and the calcify timer shrinks as
> you score. That adds the one thing every "un-losable" loop was missing: matter the core verb can't
> erase, plus a non-resetting accelerator. The full diagnosis and the portable specs for NOVA and SURGE
> are in `07-loss-mechanics.md`.
